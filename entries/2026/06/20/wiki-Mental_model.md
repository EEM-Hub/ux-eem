## Mental Models: Internal Representations of External Reality

Mental models are internal cognitive representations that people construct to understand, reason about, and predict external reality. Coined by Kenneth Craik in 1943, the concept describes how the mind builds "small-scale models" of the world to anticipate events and guide behavior. The idea has become foundational across cognitive science, psychology, human-computer interaction, system dynamics, and UX design.

## Key Concepts

- **Definition**: A mental model is an internal representation of how something works in the external world — a simplified, personal theory that shapes perception, reasoning, and decision-making.
- **Origin**: Kenneth Craik coined the term in *The Nature of Explanation* (1943); Georges-Henri Luquet's earlier work (1927) on children's internal models influenced Jean Piaget.
- **Iconic representation**: Mental models are structurally analogous to what they represent (each part of the model maps to a part of reality), similar to architects' models or physics diagrams.
- **Principle of truth**: Models typically represent only what is true in a given possibility; they can temporarily represent what is false (e.g., counterfactual reasoning).
- **Each model represents one possibility**: What is common to all the ways that possibility could occur.
- **Selective and limited**: Models are founded on incomplete information, are flexible (positively and negatively), act as information filters causing selective perception, and are constrained by working memory capacity.
- **Validation via counterexample**: People judge a conclusion valid if it holds across all modeled possibilities; they reject it when they find a counterexample where premises hold but the conclusion does not.
- **Single-loop learning**: Decisions change but the underlying mental model stays the same — the predominant and most convenient form of learning.
- **Double-loop learning**: The mental model itself is revised — shifts understanding from simple/static to broader/dynamic, accounting for environmental changes.
- **Competing theories of reasoning**: Mental model theory (Johnson-Laird & Byrne) vs. formal rules of inference, domain-specific rules, and probabilistic approaches.

## Commands and Syntax

No commands or configuration syntax are applicable — this is a cognitive science concept. However, three diagramming methods are used to externalize mental models of dynamic systems:

- **Causal loop diagrams** — show direction of information connections, causality, and feedback loops
- **System structure diagrams** — express qualitative dynamic system structure
- **Stock and flow diagrams** — quantify dynamic system structure

Tools like **Mental Modeler** (fuzzy-logic cognitive mapping) exist for capturing, analyzing, and combining individual mental models for collaborative decision-making.

## Relationships

- **Schema (psychology)**: Cognitively adjacent concept; schemas are also internal knowledge structures but are more about categorization patterns.
- **Conceptual models**: The designer's or system's intended model — UX quality depends on alignment between the user's mental model and the conceptual model.
- **Human-computer interaction / Usability**: Donald Norman and Steve Krug applied mental models extensively — mismatches between user mental models and interface design cause usability failures.
- **System dynamics and systemic thinking**: These disciplines extend and improve mental models by making them explicit, communicable, and simulatable.
- **Cognitive map / Mind map**: Related visualization techniques for externalizing mental models.
- **Map-territory relation**: Mental models are the "map" — always a simplification of the "territory" (reality).
- **OODA loop**: Decision-making framework that depends on accurate mental models of the situation.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- Kenneth Craik originated the term "mental model" in 1943; Philip Johnson-Laird and Ruth Byrne developed the formal mental model theory of reasoning.
- Mental models are **iconic** (structurally analogous to reality) and operate on a **principle of truth** (represent what is true in each possibility).
- Users reason by checking if conclusions hold across **all** modeled possibilities and use **counterexamples** to reject invalid inferences.
- Mental models are **limited by working memory** — people typically focus on a subset of possible models, often just one.
- **Single-loop learning** changes decisions within an existing model; **double-loop learning** changes the model itself — critical distinction for UX iteration and user education.
- In UX: the gap between a user's mental model and the system's conceptual model is the primary source of usability problems (Norman, Krug).
- Mental models cause **selective perception** — users literally do not see interface elements that don't fit their model of how the system works.
- Three methods externalize dynamic system mental models: causal loop diagrams, system structure diagrams, stock and flow diagrams.
