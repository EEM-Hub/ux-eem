## Don Norman: Founder of User-Centered Design and UX as a Discipline

Donald Arthur Norman (b. 1935) is an American cognitive scientist, usability engineer, and author who is one of the most influential figures in UX design. He coined the term "user-centered design" (1986), was the first person to hold the job title "User Experience Architect" (at Apple, 1993), co-founded the Nielsen Norman Group with Jakob Nielsen (1998), and authored *The Design of Everyday Things* — one of the foundational texts of the field. His career spans cognitive science research at UCSD, industry roles at Apple and HP, and the founding of The Design Lab at UC San Diego.

## Key Concepts

- **User-centered design (UCD)**: Term Norman introduced in 1986 — design based on user needs rather than secondary concerns like aesthetics. Involves simplifying task structure, making things visible, correct mapping, exploiting constraints, designing for error, and explaining affordances.
- **Human-centered design (HCD)**: Norman's revised preferred term (from the 2013 edition of DOET), defined as "an approach that puts human needs, capabilities, and behavior first, then designs to accommodate those needs, capabilities, and ways of behaving." Broader than UCD — now includes aesthetics, pleasure, and emotion.
- **Cognitive artifacts**: Artificial devices that maintain, display, or operate upon information to serve a representational function and affect human cognitive performance.
- **Affordances and the seven stages of action**: Core principles from *The Design of Everyday Things* — affordances signal what actions are possible; the seven stages of action model how users form goals, execute actions, and evaluate results.
- **Emotional design**: Norman's recognition (in his 2004 book *Emotional Design*) that aesthetics, pleasure, and emotion play critically important roles in design — a correction to his earlier stance that aesthetics were secondary.
- **Norman-Shallice model**: Framework of attentional control of executive functioning, developed with Tim Shallice. Includes the supervisory attentional system.
- **Technology-first innovation**: Norman's controversial stance that design researchers have had little impact on product innovation — technologists accomplish breakthroughs, while academics refine existing products.
- **JND (just-noticeable difference)**: Norman named his website (jnd.org) after this psychophysics concept to signify his goal of making a meaningful difference in design.

## Commands and Syntax

Not applicable — Norman's work is conceptual and theoretical rather than tool-based. However, key design principles from *The Design of Everyday Things* function as a practitioner checklist:

1. Simplify the structure of tasks
2. Make things visible
3. Get the mapping right
4. Exploit the powers of constraint
5. Design for error
6. Explain affordances
7. Apply the seven stages of action

## Relationships

- **Jakob Nielsen**: Co-founder of Nielsen Norman Group; Nielsen contributed the 10 Usability Heuristics, complementing Norman's cognitive design principles.
- **Cognitive science → UX design pipeline**: Norman's academic path (electrical engineering at MIT → mathematical psychology at UPenn → cognitive science at UCSD → Apple) models how cognitive science research translates into design practice.
- **Three Mile Island investigation**: Norman was part of the team investigating the 1979 nuclear accident — a pivotal case study in how poor interface design causes catastrophic errors, reinforcing the case for user-centered design.
- **The Design of Everyday Things**: Connects to product design (physical and digital), interaction design, and human-computer interaction as a foundational reference.
- **Emotional Design**: Extends UCD by adding the visceral, behavioral, and reflective levels of emotional response to the design framework.
- **Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g)**: The consultancy that operationalized Norman and Nielsen's research into industry-standard UX practices.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- Norman coined **"user-centered design"** in 1986 in *User Centered System Design* (co-edited with Stephen Draper).
- He later replaced "user-centered design" with **"human-centered design"** in the revised 2013 edition of *The Design of Everyday Things*, adding aesthetics and emotion to the framework.
- Norman was the **first person with "User Experience" in a job title** — as User Experience Architect at Apple (1993).
- The **Nielsen Norman Group** was founded in **1998** by Norman and Jakob Nielsen.
- Norman's key design principles: visibility, mapping, constraints, affordances, error design, and the seven stages of action.
- **Cognitive artifacts** are devices that affect cognitive performance by maintaining, displaying, or operating upon information.
- Norman investigated the **Three Mile Island accident** (1979), a canonical example of interface design failure.
- He received the **Benjamin Franklin Medal in Computer and Cognitive Science** (2006) and was elected to the **National Academy of Engineering** (2011).
- His academic background bridges **engineering** (BS/MS in EE from MIT/UPenn) and **psychology** (PhD from UPenn), which is foundational to understanding his interdisciplinary approach to design.
