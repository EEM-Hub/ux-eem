## Design Thinking: Cognitive Processes and Innovation Framework

Design thinking encompasses the cognitive, strategic, and practical procedures designers use when solving design problems. It operates as both a theory of how designers work and a teachable methodology for approaching complex, ill-defined problems. The concept spans two distinct traditions: one rooted in design cognition research (DesignThinking 1) and one popularized as a business innovation process (DesignThinking 2).

## Key Concepts

- **Wicked problems**: Problems with no definitive formulation, no true/false solution, and wide discrepancy between stakeholder perspectives. Coined by Horst Rittel in the context of design and planning. Contrasted with "tame" problems solvable through rules or technical knowledge.
- **Problem framing**: Designers do not accept problems as given — they explore, reinterpret, and restructure the problem to find a framing that suggests a route to solution.
- **Solution-focused thinking**: Designers use solution conjectures to develop understanding of the problem, in contrast to scientists' problem-focused strategies (Lawson's empirical finding, Cross's interpretation).
- **Abductive reasoning**: Designers infer possible solutions from available information and experience using non-deductive thinking (analogies, innovative abduction), as distinct from deductive or inductive reasoning.
- **Co-evolution of problem and solution**: Designer attention oscillates between understanding the problem context and generating solution ideas — new solutions deepen problem understanding, which triggers further solutions.
- **Divergent and convergent thinking**: Ideation alternates between expanding possibilities (divergent) and narrowing to select the best option (convergent).
- **Empathy**: Understanding users' psychological and emotional needs, not just physical ergonomics — the basis for innovative design per the Kelleys (IDEO/Stanford).
- **Iterative, non-linear process**: Includes context analysis, user testing, problem finding, ideation, sketching, prototyping, and evaluation — not a strict sequence.

## Commands and Syntax

No CLI commands. The key procedural frameworks are:

**Plattner/Meinel/Leifer five-phase model:**
1. (Re)define the problem
2. Needfinding and benchmarking
3. Ideating
4. Building
5. Testing

**Three overlapping spaces (Brown/IDEO):**
1. Inspiration — observe real-world behavior, identify problems/opportunities, document in a brief with constraints and objectives
2. Ideation — brainstorm, diverge/converge, find patterns, synthesize insights
3. Implementation — prototype, test, evaluate, iterate, refine

**Prototyping practice:** Turn ideas into concrete artifacts (rough mock-ups to detailed prototypes) to gather feedback, identify strengths/weaknesses, and prompt new ideas quickly.

## Relationships

- **User Research & Empathic Design**: Design thinking's empathy phase directly feeds UX research methods — personas, contextual inquiry, user interviews
- **Participatory Design**: Rittel's "second generation" design methods emphasize stakeholder participation, connecting to co-design and collaborative UX practices
- **Systems Thinking**: Related approach for understanding interconnected problem spaces; design thinking is more solution-focused while systems thinking is more analytical
- **Lateral Thinking**: Complementary creative reasoning strategy for generating non-obvious solutions
- **Reflective Practice**: Schön's concept of reflection-in-action underpins the iterative, self-correcting nature of design thinking
- **Service Design**: Design thinking extended beyond products to designing service experiences
- **Human-Centered Design**: Rose in the 1980s alongside design thinking; shares emphasis on user needs but is more specifically methodological
- **Design Methods**: The broader field from which design thinking emerged; includes systematic and intuitive approaches

## Exam-Relevant Points

- **Two traditions**: DesignThinking 1 (design cognition, from 1960s research) vs. DesignThinking 2 (business innovation process, popularized 2000s). Nigel Cross formalized this distinction.
- **Wicked problems** were introduced by Horst Rittel and Melvin Webber (1973) — no definitive formulation, no stopping rule, no true/false test for solutions.
- **Key figures and dates**: Peter Rowe's *Design Thinking* (1987) was the first significant use of the term in design literature. John E. Arnold's *Creative Engineering* (1959) and L. Bruce Archer's *Systematic Method for Designers* (1963–64) were among the earliest writings. Stanford d.school founded 2003. IDEO formed 1991. First design thinking research symposium at Delft University 1991.
- **Abductive reasoning** (from Peirce) is the primary reasoning mode in design — distinct from deduction and induction.
- **Solution-focused vs. problem-focused**: Lawson's study showed architects use solution-focused strategies while scientists use problem-focused ones.
- **Criticisms**: Kimbell — "de-politicizing managerial practice"; Vinsel — "'creative confidence' without actual capabilities"; Iskander — reaffirms "privileged role of the designer," ignores power dynamics in empathy; Ackermann — elevates designer into "spiritual medium" superseding domain expertise.
- **Arnold's four areas of creative thinking**: novel functionality, higher performance, lower production costs, increased salability.
- **Co-evolution** is a defining characteristic — problem and solution develop together, not sequentially.
- **Prototyping** is core to implementation — enables quick identification of strengths/weaknesses and prompts new ideas.
