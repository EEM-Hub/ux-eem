## Typography: The Art and Technique of Arranging Type

Typography is the art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and visually appealing. It encompasses selecting typefaces, point sizes, line lengths, line spacing, letter spacing, and kerning. Once a specialized occupation, the Digital Age democratized typography, making it accessible to anyone with a personal computer—though this has also diluted the application of principles developed over generations of skilled practitioners.

## Key Concepts

- **Typography vs. Type Design**: Most typographers do not design typefaces; some type designers do not consider themselves typographers. These are related but distinct crafts.
- **Legibility vs. Readability**: Legibility refers to *perception*—how easily individual characters are distinguished. Readability refers to *comprehension*—how easily text is read as a whole. Both are separate but related.
- **Type Color**: The overall density of ink on the page, determined by typeface, word spacing, leading, and margin depth—not literal color.
- **Bouma / Word Shape**: Some typographers argue that overall word shape (influenced by ascenders and descenders in lowercase) is essential to readability, contrasting with parallel letter recognition theory.
- **Saccadic Rhythm**: The eye reads in jumps of approximately three words; lines requiring more than three or four saccadic jumps introduce strain and reading errors.
- **All-Caps Reduces Legibility**: Uppercase-only text renders words indistinguishable as groups, requiring extra effort to parse.
- **Font vs. Typeface**: A *typeface* is the design family; a *font* is a specific style/weight within it. Common conflation traces to Steve Jobs mislabeling them for Apple in 1984.
- **Experimental Typography**: Unconventional approaches prioritizing emotional expression over legibility (e.g., David Carson's *Ray Gun* magazine work in the 1990s).
- **Serif vs. Sans-Serif**: Serif typefaces are generally preferred for body text and long-form reading; sans-serif is common for headings, short articles, and introductory paragraphs.

## Commands and Syntax

Not applicable (this is a conceptual/historical topic, not a technical tooling page). However, key typographic parameters include:

- **Point size** — character height measurement
- **Leading** — vertical space between lines (e.g., 14pt on 1.4× leading)
- **Tracking** — uniform letter-spacing adjustment (e.g., +0.2pt extra tracking)
- **Kerning** — spacing adjustment between specific letter pairs
- **Line length** — optimal ~10 words per line for readability
- **Justification** — aligned right edge vs. ragged right; justified copy requires careful adjustment to maintain readability

## Relationships

- **History of Printing**: Typography is inseparable from the evolution of movable type—from Bi Sheng's ceramic type (Song dynasty, ~1040), to Korean metal type (~1230), to Gutenberg's lead-alloy press (1439).
- **Calligraphy and Lettering**: Early typefaces (blackletter/Gothic) directly imitated hand-lettering styles of scribes; Roman typefaces evolved from Greek and Roman lapidary letter carving.
- **Graphic Design**: Typography is a core component of graphic design, editorial design, and publication design. Style guides standardize typeface usage across publications.
- **Orthography and Linguistics**: Typesetting conventions are shaped by language-specific rules (e.g., French non-breaking space before colons).
- **Digital Design**: Web pages, mobile screens, LCDs, and video games have expanded typography's application domain since digitization.
- **Industrial Design**: Typography appears on appliances, vehicle dashboards, signage, and wearables.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- Typography involves selecting **typefaces, point sizes, line lengths, line spacing, letter spacing, and kerning**.
- **Legibility = perception** (can you distinguish characters); **Readability = comprehension** (can you understand the text). A legible typeface can be unreadable through poor layout, and vice versa.
- **Three fundamental principles**: legibility, readability, and aesthetics.
- Movable type was invented by **Bi Sheng** in **11th-century Song dynasty China** (ceramic); **Korea** introduced **metal movable type ~1230**; **Gutenberg** (1439) brought lead-alloy movable type and the mechanical press to Europe.
- The eye processes approximately **three words per saccadic jump**; lines requiring more than 3–4 jumps cause reading errors.
- **All-caps text reduces legibility** because it eliminates word-shape differentiation.
- **Type color** refers to ink density on the page, not chromatic color.
- **Old-style Roman** typefaces have similarly weighted lines; **modern Roman** typefaces show contrast between light and heavy strokes.
- The **International Typographic Style** (1920s–30s) and **Swiss Style** (1950s–60s) are key movements in typographic history.
- Digital typography's "democratization" began in the mid-1980s with personal computers and tools like **Fontographer**.
