## User Experience Design — Foundations, Elements, and Practice

User experience design (UX design, UXD, UED, XD) is a user-centered design discipline that defines the entire experience a person has when interacting with a company, its services, and products. Rooted in human factors and ergonomics since the late 1940s, UX design goes beyond user interface design to encompass usability, usefulness, desirability, brand perception, and overall performance. The term was coined by Don Norman in the early 1990s to capture the full scope of a person's experience with a system — beyond just the interface.

## Key Concepts

- **UX vs. UI**: UI design focuses solely on the computer interface; UX design encompasses all aspects of a user's perceived experience with a product, including usability, usefulness, desirability, and brand perception.
- **UX as a subset of CX**: UX design is an element of the broader customer experience (CX), covering all design aspects and stages around a customer's experience.
- **User-centered design approach**: Research, data analysis, and test results drive design decisions rather than aesthetic preferences and opinions.
- **Don Norman coined the term**: He found "human interface" and "usability" too narrow — he wanted to cover industrial design, graphics, the interface, physical interaction, and the manual.
- **Multidisciplinary discipline**: UX design draws from human-computer interaction, interaction design, visual design, information architecture, user research, motion graphics, animation, programming, and service design.
- **Usability is necessary but not sufficient**: Good usability is essential to positive UX but does not alone guarantee it. Usability is a subset of the broader user experience.

## Elements of UX Design

- **User Research**: Systematic study of target users via interviews, surveys, field studies, and contextual inquiry. Grounds design in evidence rather than assumptions. Produces personas and scenarios.
- **Visual Design**: The aesthetics and look-and-feel of the interface. Uses colors, images, and symbols to convey messages. Informed by Gestalt psychology and visual perception principles.
- **Information Architecture (IA)**: The art and science of structuring and organizing information to support usability and findability. Concerned with metadata, page structure, and navigation.
- **Interaction Design (IxD)**: Centers on the interaction between users and products, aiming to create efficient, delightful experiences. Has evolved from specifying UI components to designing contextual interfaces based on user needs.
- **Usability**: The extent to which specified users can achieve specified goals with effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction in a specified context of use (per ISO standards).
- **Accessibility**: Ease of reach, use, and understanding. WCAG principles: Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust (POUR).
- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)**: Design, evaluation, and implementation of interactive computing systems for human use.

## Design Process

- **Research phase**: Empathize with users; identify assumptions, commonalities, needs, goals, and mental models.
- **User modeling**: Create personas — composite archetypes based on behavior patterns uncovered during research.
- **Wireframing**: Sketch interaction frameworks defining high-level screen layouts, product flow, behavior, and organization.
- **Visual design framework**: Define experience attributes, visual language, and visual style in parallel with wireframing.
- **Pixel-perfect implementation**: Translating wireframes to full-resolution screens at the pixel level, requiring close designer-developer collaboration.

## UX Deliverables

- Core specification documents: title page, introduction to the feature, wireframes, version history.
- Extended deliverables: flow models, cultural models, personas, user stories, scenarios, prototypes, user flow diagrams, mockups, presentations, annotated wireframes, tech docs.
- Post-launch activities: usability testing, A/B testing, information architecture review, sitemap and user flow updates, additional wireframing based on test results.

## UX Stakeholders and Roles

- **UX Designer**: The primary practitioner role.
- **Interaction Designer (IxD)**: Specifies product behavior; works with visual and industrial designers.
- **Visual Designer**: Ensures visual representation communicates data, expected behavior, and brand ideals; aims for maximum usability + maximum desirability.
- **UI Designer**: Focuses on the look and style of software interfaces (graphical, voice-controlled, etc.).
- **UX Writer**: Crafts microcopy (button labels, error messages, tooltips), conducts user research, sets product tone, ensures accessibility.
- **Technical Communicator**: Couples writing/communication skills with UX design for multi-platform content.
- **Other roles**: User experience researcher, information architect, human factors engineer, business analyst, usability specialist, creative director.

## UX Design Testing

- **Usability testing** is the most common method — testing the design, not the user.
- Tests should be conducted early and often, following Agile development principles.
- Both qualitative (live user testing) and quantitative (A/B testing, loggers, mobile agents) methods are used.
- Personas inform testing by representing the most common users and predicting their interactions.

## Relationships

- **User-centered design** is the foundational philosophy underlying UX design.
- **Human-computer interaction** and **human factors/ergonomics** are the academic roots.
- **Interaction design** is an essential component, focusing on user-product interaction.
- **Information architecture** structures the content that UX makes accessible.
- **Usability** is a subset of UX — necessary but not sufficient.
- **Customer experience (CX)** is the broader context; UX is one element within it.
- **Accessibility / WCAG** ensures inclusive design.
- **Agile** and **design thinking** influence UX process and iteration.
- **Don Norman** is the field's central historical figure.

## Exam-Relevant Points

- UX design is NOT the same as UI design — UX encompasses all aspects of the user's experience, while UI focuses only on the interface.
- Don Norman coined "user experience" because "human interface" and "usability" were too narrow.
- The field's roots are in human factors and ergonomics (since the late 1940s), not graphic design.
- UX design is a subset of CX; usability is a subset of UX.
- WCAG's four principles: **Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust (POUR)**.
- Core UX deliverables: title page, feature introduction, wireframes, version history.
- Usability testing tests **the design**, not the user.
- Personas are composite archetypes based on **behavior patterns**, not demographic profiles.
- Research, data, and testing drive UX decisions — not aesthetic preferences.
- The design process involves parallel tracks: interaction framework (wireframes) and visual design framework.
- Pixel-perfect precision means the product appears exactly as intended by the designer.
