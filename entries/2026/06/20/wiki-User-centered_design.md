## User-Centered Design (UCD)

User-centered design is a framework of processes that gives extensive attention to usability goals, user characteristics, environment, tasks, and workflow at every stage of the design process. Coined by Rob Kling in 1977 and popularized by Don Norman's books, UCD optimizes products around how users actually engage rather than forcing users to adapt their behavior to the product.

## Key Concepts

- **UCD definition**: A design framework where users, their needs, tasks, and environment are central throughout the entire product lifecycle — from requirements through post-production
- **Core philosophy**: Optimize the product around user behavior rather than forcing users to change their behavior to accommodate the product
- **Priority hierarchy**: Users first, then product context/objectives/operating environment, then granular task details
- **Cognitive science foundation**: UCD applies cognitive science principles to create intuitive, efficient products by understanding users' mental processes, behaviors, and needs
- **Testing rationale**: Designers struggle to understand first-time user experiences and learning curves; testing at every stage mitigates this gap
- **Norman's four design principles**:
  - Simplify task structure so actions are intuitive at any moment
  - Make things visible: conceptual model, actions, results, and feedback
  - Achieve correct mappings between intended results and required actions
  - Embrace and exploit system constraints
- **Personas**: Archetypal user representations synthesized from ethnographic interviews, captured in 1-2 page descriptions covering behavior patterns, goals, skills, attitudes, and environment
- **Distinction from related approaches**: UCD differs from use-centered design and usage-centered design; compare also with human-centered design (HCD)

## Commands and Syntax

No CLI commands. The UCD process follows a four-phase cycle:

1. **Specify context of use** — Identify primary users, their reasons for use, requirements, and environment
2. **Specify requirements** — Identify detailed technical requirements; aids planning and goal-setting
3. **Create design solutions and development** — Iterative cycle of product testing and refinement based on goals and requirements
4. **Evaluate product** — Usability testing and user feedback collection at every design stage

This cycle repeats iteratively. Can be combined with waterfall, agile, or any software engineering methodology.

**Research methods used in UCD:**
- Investigative: ethnographic study, contextual inquiry, prototype testing, usability testing
- Generative: card sorting, affinity diagramming, participatory design sessions
- Analytical: comparative analysis of similar usable products

## Relationships

- **Related design traditions**: Cooperative design (Scandinavian, since 1970), participatory design (North American, since 1990), contextual design (customer-centered, real-world data)
- **Parent concept**: Human-centered design (HCD) — UCD is a specific instantiation
- **Key overlap**: Interaction design, user experience design, usability engineering
- **Tension with**: Activity-centered design (focuses on activities rather than users), use-centered design, usage-centered design
- **Norman's evolution**: His later book *Emotional Design* revisits and corrects what he considered overly reductive positions in *The Design of Everyday Things* — notably the dismissal of aesthetics as secondary
- **Standards**: ISO 13407:1999 (human-centred design processes) superseded by ISO 9241-210:2010 (human-centred design for interactive systems)

## Exam-Relevant Points

- UCD was coined by **Rob Kling in 1977**, not by Don Norman (common misconception)
- Norman's two key books: *User-Centered System Design* (1986) and *The Design of Everyday Things* (1988)
- The **six UCD principles**: explicit understanding of users/tasks/environments; user involvement throughout; user-centered evaluation drives design; iterative process; addresses whole user experience; multidisciplinary team
- The four-phase process is **iterative and repeatable**, not linear — the phases cycle until the product meets usability goals
- Personas are synthesized from **ethnographic interviews with real people**, not invented from assumptions
- UCD's distinguishing characteristic vs. other philosophies: it optimizes the product around user behavior rather than requiring behavioral adaptation
- ISO 9241-210:2010 is the current governing standard for human-centred design of interactive systems
- Cooperative design originated in **Scandinavia (1970s)**; participatory design is its **North American** adaptation (1990s)
