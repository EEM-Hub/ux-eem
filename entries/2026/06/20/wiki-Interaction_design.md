## Interaction Design (IxD): Foundations, Methods, and Dimensions

Interaction design (IxD) is the practice of designing interactive digital products, environments, systems, and services. Unlike engineering disciplines that focus on technical implementation, IxD focuses primarily on **behavior** — how a product responds to and supports user actions. It synthesizes possibilities rather than analyzing existing states, drawing from psychology, human-computer interaction, information architecture, and user research to create designs tailored to user needs within business and technical constraints.

## Key Concepts

- **IxD focuses on behavior**, not just form — distinguishing it from other design fields and from science/engineering
- **Cognitive friction**: when an interface is complex, inconsistent, and behaves unexpectedly across different modes (coined by Alan Cooper)
- **Personas**: archetypes describing user goals and observed behavior patterns; they encapsulate behavioral data in a way designers and stakeholders can understand and relate to
- **Cognitive dimensions framework**: a lightweight vocabulary for evaluating design quality (consistency, error-proneness, hard mental operations, viscosity, premature commitment)
- **Affective interaction design**: designing for emotional responses — products must convey positive emotions and avoid negative ones; affective aspects influence perceived usability
- **Five dimensions of IxD**: Words (1D), Visual representations (2D), Physical objects/space (3D), Time (4D), Behavior (5D — added by Kevin Silver)
- **Goal-directed design**: satisfying user needs and desires rather than serving provider interests (Alan Cooper's approach)
- **Usability**: the quality attribute describing how usable an interface is (Jakob Nielsen's definition)
- **Shneiderman's Eight Golden Rules**: well-known heuristics for creating usable interface systems
- Interaction designers manage **design complexity** — many possible users, contexts, and software states
- Term coined by **Bill Moggridge and Bill Verplank** in the mid-1980s; Moggridge originally called it "soft-face" (1984)

## Commands and Syntax

No CLI commands. Key tools and frameworks referenced:

- **Figma, Adobe XD**: widely used interaction design tools for managing design complexity
- **Cognitive dimensions framework**: used to evaluate and modify designs via "design maneuvers" that alter a design within a particular dimension
- **Persona creation process**: encapsulate critical behavioral data using storytelling to engage social and emotional aspects
- **Emotional design models**: Don Norman's emotional design model, Patrick Jordan's pleasure model, McCarthy & Wright's Technology as Experience framework

## Relationships

- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)**: academic research foundation; provides methods like cognitive dimensions and cognitive walkthrough for testing usability
- **Information Architecture**: IxD borrows structural organization principles from IA
- **User Experience (UX) Design**: IxD practitioners often work as UX designers; IxD is a core discipline within UX
- **User Interface (UI) Design**: IxD evolved from the term "user interface design"; both concern system interfaces but IxD emphasizes behavior over time
- **Industrial Design**: shares core principles (form, color, aesthetics, human perception); IxD originated as industrial design applied to software
- **Cognitive Psychology**: provides foundational concepts — mental models, mapping, interface metaphors, affordances (Donald Norman's *The Design of Everyday Things*)
- **Human Factors & Ergonomics**: grounds IxD in anthropometry, biomechanics, physiology, and psychology
- **Architecture**: increasingly relevant as IxD expands into ubiquitous computing, urban informatics, and context-aware environments
- **Design Research**: iterative cycles of user research identify needs, motivations, and behaviors

## Exam-Relevant Points

- IxD was coined by **Bill Moggridge and Bill Verplank** in the **mid-1980s**; Moggridge's earlier term was "soft-face" (1984)
- First academic IxD program: **Carnegie Mellon University, 1994** (Master of Design in Interaction Design)
- The **five dimensions**: Words, Visual Representations, Physical Objects/Space, Time, Behavior (the 5th added by Kevin Silver to Crampton Smith's original four)
- **Alan Cooper's** key concepts: goal-directed design, cognitive friction (*The Inmates Are Running the Asylum*)
- **Jakob Nielsen** defines usability as a "quality attribute"
- **Shneiderman's Eight Golden Rules** are a standard heuristic set for usable interface design
- **Personas** use storytelling to help designers visualize optimal product behavior (Goodwin, 2009)
- **Cognitive dimensions** provide a lightweight vocabulary (not deep analysis) for evaluating notation, UI, or programming language design
- **Affective aspects** influence perceived usability — emotional design is not merely aesthetic
- **IxDA** (Interaction Design Association) founded 2003, 80,000+ members, 173+ local groups; Interaction Awards ended August 2024
- Key institutions: MIT Visible Language Workshop (1975, Muriel Cooper), NYU ITP (1979), RCA Computer-Related Design MA (1990, Gillian Crampton Smith), Interaction Design Institute Ivrea (2001), CIID Copenhagen (2007)
- IxD is a **design field** (synthesizes possibilities), not a science or engineering field (which analyze what exists)
