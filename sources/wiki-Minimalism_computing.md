In [computing](./Computing), **minimalism** is the application of [minimalist](./Minimalist) philosophies and principles in the design and use of [hardware](./Computer_hardware) and [software](./Software). Minimalism, in this sense, means designing systems that use the least hardware and software resources possible.

 

## History

 

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, programmers worked within the confines of relatively expensive and limited [resources](./Resource_(computer_science)) of common platforms. Eight or sixteen [kilobytes](./Kilobyte) of [RAM](./Random-access_memory) was common; 64 kilobytes was considered a vast amount and was the entire [address space](./Address_space) accessible to the [8-bit](./8-bit) CPUs predominant during the earliest generations of [personal computers](./Personal_computer). The most common storage medium was the 5.25 inch [floppy disk](./Floppy_disk) holding from 88 to 170 kilobytes. Hard drives with capacities from five to ten [megabytes](./Megabyte) cost thousands of dollars.

 

Over time, personal-computer memory capacities expanded by orders of magnitude and mainstream programmers took advantage of the added storage to increase their software's capabilities and to make development easier by using [higher-level languages](./High-level_programming_language). By contrast, [system requirements](./System_requirements) for [legacy software](./Legacy_software) remained the same. As a result, even the most elaborate, feature-rich programs of yesteryear seem minimalist in comparison with current software.

 

One example of a program whose system requirements once gave it a heavyweight reputation is the [GNU Emacs](./GNU_Emacs) text editor, which gained the [backronym](./Backronym) "Eight Megabytes And Constantly Swapping" in an era when 8 megabytes was a lot of RAM.[[1]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-1) Today, Emacs' mainly textual [buffer](./Data_buffer)-based paradigm uses far fewer resources than [desktop metaphor](./Desktop_metaphor) [GUI](./GUI) [IDEs](./Integrated_development_environment) with comparable features such as [Eclipse](./Eclipse_(software)) or [Netbeans](./Netbeans).[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed)*] In a speech at the 2002 International Lisp Conference, [Richard Stallman](./Richard_Stallman) indicated that minimalism was a concern in his development of [GNU](./GNU) and Emacs, based on his experiences with [Lisp](./Lisp_(programming_language)) and system specifications of low-end [minicomputers](./Minicomputer) at the time.[[2]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-2)

 

As the capabilities and system requirements of common desktop software and operating systems grew throughout the 1980s and 1990s, and as software development became dominated by teams espousing conflicting, faddish [software development methodologies](./Software_development_methodologies), some developers adopted minimalism as a philosophy and chose to limit their programs to a predetermined size or scope.[[3]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-3)[*[non-primary source needed](./Wikipedia:No_original_research#Primary,_secondary_and_tertiary_sources)*] A focus on [software optimization](./Software_optimization) can result in minimalist software, as programmers reduce the number of operations their program carries out in order to speed execution.[[4]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-4)[*[non-primary source needed](./Wikipedia:No_original_research#Primary,_secondary_and_tertiary_sources)*]

 

In the early 21st century, new developments in computing have brought minimalism to the forefront. In what has been termed the [post-PC era](./Post-PC_era) it is no longer necessary to buy a high-end personal computer merely to perform common computing tasks.[[5]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-5) [Mobile computing](./Mobile_computing) devices, such as [smartphones](./Smartphones), [tablet computers](./Tablet_computers), [netbooks](./Netbooks) and [plug computers](./Plug_computer), often have smaller memory capacities, less-capable graphics subsystems, and slower processors when compared to the personal computer they are expected to replace. In addition, heavy use of graphics effects like [alpha blending](./Alpha_blending) drains the battery faster than a "flat ui".[[6]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-6) The growing popularity of these devices has made minimalism an important design concern. 

 [![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Windows_8.1_Calculator.png/250px-Windows_8.1_Calculator.png)](./File:Windows_8.1_Calculator.png)The mimimalist design of [Windows Calculator](./Windows_Calculator) in Windows 8 

In [Windows 8](./Windows_8), [Microsoft](./Microsoft) implemented the "simple, squared-off" [Metro](./Metro_(design_language)) appearance, which was less graphics-intensive than the previous [Aero](./Windows_Aero) interface used in [Windows 7](./Windows_7) and [Windows Vista](./Windows_Vista). This change was made in part because of the rise of smaller, battery-powered devices and the need to conserve power.[[7]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-7)[[8]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-8)[[9]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-9) Version 7 of [Apple](./Apple_Inc.)'s [iOS](./IOS) made similar changes for [user experience](./User_experience) reasons.[[10]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-10)

 

[Google](./Google)'s [Chrome browser](./Google_Chrome) and [ChromeOS](./ChromeOS) are often cited as examples of minimalist design.[[11]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-11)[[12]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-12)

 

## Usage

 

Developers may create [user interfaces](./User_interface) to be as simple as possible by eliminating [buttons](./Button_(computing)) and [dialog boxes](./Dialog_box) that may potentially confuse the user. Minimalism is sometimes used in its [visual arts meaning](./Minimalism), particularly in the [industrial design](./Industrial_design) of the hardware device or [software theme](./Theme_(computing)).

 

Some developers have attempted to create programs to perform a particular function in the fewest lines of code, or smallest compiled executable size possible on a given platform.[[13]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-13)[[14]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-14) Some [Linux](./Linux) distributions mention minimalism as a goal. [Alpine](./Alpine_Linux), [Arch](./Arch_Linux), [Puppy](./Puppy_Linux), [Bodhi](./Bodhi_Linux), [CrunchBang](./CrunchBang_Linux), [dynebolic](./Dynebolic)[[15]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-15) and [Tiny Core](./Tiny_Core_Linux) are examples. The early development of the [Unix](./Unix) system occurred on low-powered hardware, and [Dennis Ritchie](./Dennis_Ritchie) and [Ken Thompson](./Ken_Thompson) have stated their opinion that this constraint contributed to the system's "elegance of design".[[16]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-16)

 

[Programming language](./Programming_language) designers can create minimal programming languages by eschewing [syntactic sugar](./Syntactic_sugar) and extensive [library functions](./Programming_library). Such languages may be [Turing tarpits](./Turing_tarpit) due to not offering standard support for common programming tasks. Creating a minimal Lisp [interpreter](./Interpreter_(computing)) is a common learning task set before [computer science](./Computer_science) students.[[17]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-17) The [Lambda calculus](./Lambda_calculus), developed by [Alonzo Church](./Alonzo_Church) is a minimal programming language that uses only function definitions and function applications.[[18]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-18)[[19]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-19) [Scheme](./Scheme_(programming_language)),[[20]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-20)[[21]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-21) [Forth](./Forth_(programming_language)),[[22]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-22) and [Go](./Go_(programming_language))[[23]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-23)[[24]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-24) are cited as examples of practical, minimal programming languages.

 

The programming hobby of [code golf](./Code_golf) results in minimalist software,[[25]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-25) but these are typically exercises or [code poetry](./Code_poetry), not usable applications software.

 

[John Millar Carroll](./John_Millar_Carroll), in his book *Minimalism Beyond the [Nürnberg Funnel](./Nuremberg_Funnel)* pointed out that the use of minimalism results in "instant-use" devices such as video games, [ATMs](./Automated_teller_machine), [voting machines](./Voting_machine), and [mall kiosks](./Electronic_kiosk) with little-or-no [learning curve](./Learning_curve) that do not require the user to read manuals.[[26]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-26) User Interface researchers have performed experiments suggesting that minimalism, as illustrated by the design principles of [parsimony](./Occam's_razor) and [transparency](./Transparency_(human–computer_interaction)), bolsters efficiency and learnability.[[27]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-27) Minimalism is implicit in the [Unix philosophies](./Unix_philosophy) of "everything is a text stream" and "do one thing and do it well", although modern Unix/Linux distributions do not hold so rigorously to this philosophy.[[28]](./Minimalism_(computing)#cite_note-28)
