In [page layout](./Page_layout) and [illustration](./Illustration), **white space** (or often **[negative space](./Negative_space)**) is the portion of a page left unmarked: [margins](./Margin_(typography)), [gutters](./Gutter_(typography)), and space between columns, lines of type, graphics, figures, or objects drawn or depicted. 
It is not necessarily actually white if the background is of a different colour. The term arises from [graphic design](./Graphic_design) practice, where printing processes generally use white paper.[[1]](./White_space_(visual_arts)#cite_note-1) White space may be affirmatively an element of design rather than just space left blank.

 

When space is at a premium, such as in some types of magazine, newspaper, and yellow pages advertising, white space is restricted in order to get as much information onto the page as possible. A page full of text or graphics with very little white space may appear cluttered, and be difficult to read.[[2]](./White_space_(visual_arts)#cite_note-2) Some designs compensate for restricted white space by use of [leading](./Leading) and [typeface](./Typeface). Extensive white space may be left intentionally, with the purpose of giving an appearance deemed classic, elegant, or rich.
